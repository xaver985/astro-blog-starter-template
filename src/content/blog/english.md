I taught myself English. Looking back, the method hardly changed over the years. I would take a text, read it until I could read it fluently, recite it, write it out from memory, and translate it. What changed was the material: New Concept English in secondary school, then novels and exam passages at university.

## New Concept English

Starting *New Concept English* in junior high school was my family's suggestion. I finished Book 2 in junior high, and Books 3 and 4 in senior high school. I studied all of them on my own.

## Four steps for every lesson

I treated each lesson the same way.

1. **Read it until it is fluent.** I read the text over and over until I could read it smoothly.
2. **Recite it.** I learned the whole text by heart.
3. **Write it out from memory.** With the book closed, I wrote the text down, then checked it against the original.
4. **Translate it.** I translated the text from English into Chinese. When I got something wrong, I checked it against the original, corrected it, and memorised the right translation.

## Novels and TEM-8 passages at university

At university I started reading novels in the original English. I read part of *The Great Gatsby* and read *Nineteen Eighty-Four* once. *The Lord of the Rings* I have read three or four times. It left the deepest impression on me, and it is still my favourite English novel. The descriptions are rich in detail, the world is vast, and the whole book reads like an epic.

I also kept using the four steps on a new kind of material: the reading passages from past TEM-8 papers. TEM-8 (Test for English Majors, Band 8) is the highest level of the national English exam for English majors in China. Using these passages was also my family's suggestion. I recited these passages, wrote them out from memory and translated them, just as I had done with New Concept English.

Most of the texts in *New Concept English* Book 4 are argumentative essays by well-known writers. The TEM-8 passages are far more varied: argumentative essays, literary prose, fiction, informal essays and more. They are also much longer, and most of them are harder to understand than the texts in Book 4.

## Read a lot

I personally don't think that one could study English well merely by grinding loads of problem sets. You have to read, throw yourself into the world of the English language, and immerse yourself in it. Only in this way are you able to think like native speakers.
